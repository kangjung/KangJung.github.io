01START SMALL

A little at a time.
Not all at once.

A large library does not need a large commitment. Choose a small batch and make one decision at a time. Twenty is a session size, not a daily limit.

  1. 01

    Choose your batch

    Review 20, 50 or 100 photos. Start with all photos, screenshots, this month’s photos or photos more than a year old.

    2050100
  2. 02

    Swipe, then decide

    Left marks a photo for deletion; right keeps it. Undo your previous choice when needed. Swiping alone never deletes a photo.

  3. 03

    Take one last look

    See your candidates together. Keep anything you change your mind about, then choose trash or permanent deletion.

02TWO WAYS TO FIND

Similar photos.
Same filenames.

The two criteria stay separate, so you always know why a group was found.

A

Similar photos

Photos taken close together are compared by visual features. Use a recent preset or choose start and end dates, then keep selected photos or mark the whole group for final review.

Same-name photos

Find exact filename matches across dates and folders. Compare the folder, dimensions, capture time and file size before choosing what to keep.

The same filename does not mean the photo content or file bytes are identical.

03YOU MAKE THE FINAL CALL

A choice,
not a deletion.

Candidate selection and actual photo removal are separate steps.

  • Mark a whole group

    If none are needed, send the entire group to deletion candidates. The latest group choice can be undone.

  • Trash or permanent deletion

    Trash is the default. Permanent deletion is a separate choice, and PicKip cannot restore it.

  • Final review before deletion

    Review every candidate and switch any photo back to Keep before confirming the final action.

04PROCESSED ON YOUR DEVICE

Your photos stay local.
Your choices stay yours.

No signup or photo uploads. Similarity analysis, filename checks, keep choices and cleanup records are processed and stored on your device.

Does swiping left delete a photo immediately?

No. It only marks the photo as a deletion candidate. Nothing is removed until you confirm it on the final review screen.

What is the difference between Similar photos and Same-name photos?

Similar photos compares the visual patterns of photos taken close together. Same-name photos groups exact filename matches across dates and folders.

Does the same filename mean the files are identical?

No. Two different photos can share a filename. Review the thumbnails, folders, dimensions, capture times and file sizes before choosing what to keep.

Can I mark an entire similar-photo group?

Yes. You can send the whole group to deletion candidates, undo that choice, and review everything again before any deletion.

Why does PicKip need photo access?

It reads photo lists, capture times, filenames and thumbnails to show and compare photos. With selected-photo access, only the photos you allow are available.

Is PicKip available on iPhone?

PicKip is currently Android-only and supports Korean, English and Japanese.
